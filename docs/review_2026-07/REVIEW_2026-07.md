## 0. State of the suite

The platform is a six-package Python monorepo implementing a full mission-operations training chain for a fictitious 6U EO CubeSat (EO-Sat-1 / EOSAT-1, 450 km SSO, 98°):

| Package | Role |
|---|---|
| `smo-common` | SGP4 propagator, eclipse/contact geometry, ECSS/CCSDS packet build and decommutation, pydantic-validated config loaders |
| `smo-simulator` | Spacecraft: 6 subsystem models, PUS dispatcher (S1/2/3/5/6/8/9/11/12/13/15/17/**18**/19/20), FDIR, failure manager, 31 instructor scenarios, breakpoints, instructor console |
| `smo-mcs` | Mission control system: ~70 routes, TM decode, TC build/verification, procedure runner (64 procedures), displays, go/no-go, alarm journal, SQLite archive |
| `smo-planner` | Mission planning system: contact windows, activity scheduling, imaging targets, power/data budgets, constraint checkers |
| `smo-rfsim` | Baseband: RS(255,223)/Viterbi/BCH/ASM/CLTU chain, DSP mod-demod, channel model, GNU-Radio bridge |
| `smo-gateway` | TM/TC byte relay, one upstream sim, N downstream clients |

Ground segment: Iqaluit (63.75° N, uOttawa) and Troll (−72.01° S, KSAT lease). The suite is the operations companion to the SpaceCDF design tool and the 546-page course textbook.

## 2. How to test all functionality

### 2.1 Framework and how it runs

pytest + pytest-asyncio. `tests/conftest.py` injects every `packages/smo-*/src` onto `sys.path` (belt-and-braces over the editable install — DI-28), exposes a `config_dir` fixture pointing at `configs/eosat1`, and a `mock_engine`/`service_dispatcher` pair for dispatcher unit tests. `tests/test_acceptance/conftest.py` builds an `RFTestHarness` that runs a **real `SimulationEngine` plus a real RF `PipelineCoordinator`** (TX → channel → RX), so acceptance tests exercise genuine signal processing rather than a mock.

Two suites are excluded from routine runs: `tests/test_e2e_browser` (35 Playwright tests, real browser, mission passes 01–13) and `tests/test_integration/test_commissioning_rf.py` (needs the GNU-Radio bridge).

**Recommended invocation pattern** (each chunk finishes well inside a 45 s shell budget except acceptance, which takes ~2.5 min):

```
pytest tests/test_simulator
pytest tests/test_mcs tests/test_planner tests/test_common tests/test_gateway tests/test_commissioning
pytest tests/test_integration -k "not commissioning_rf"
pytest tests/test_rfsim
pytest tests/test_acceptance          # slowest; RF harness
pytest tests --ignore=<the above>     # top-level regression files
```

### 4.3 Why the gaps are bimodal

The distribution has two clear modes, at ~40 min and ~85 min, and this is expected orbital mechanics for a two-station polar network rather than a residual defect:

- **~40 min mode (14 gaps in the 30–60 min bin, 4 more at 15–30 min).** Iqaluit sits at +63.75° latitude and Troll at −72.01°. On a 94.5-min near-polar orbit (98° inclination) the spacecraft crosses the northern polar region and the southern polar region **half an orbit apart**. When the ground track happens to fall inside both stations' visibility circles on the same revolution, contact alternates Troll → Iqaluit (or the reverse) at roughly half-orbit spacing: 47.2 min minus the two pass durations (mean 6.8 and 8.1 min) ≈ **32–40 min of gap**.
- **~85 min mode (18 gaps in the 60–90 min bin).** Earth rotates 23.6° of longitude under the orbit per revolution. On revolutions where the ascending or descending node has walked outside one station's longitude window, that station is missed entirely and the next contact is with the *same* station one full orbit later: 94.5 min minus a pass duration ≈ **86 min**. That is the median (84.3 min), and the median/half-orbit ratio of 1.79 is the direct measure of how often one of the two stations is skipped.
- **The two 120+ min outliers (max 147.5 min)** are revolutions where *both* stations are missed — the node has drifted such that neither visibility circle is crossed on that pass. At 5° masks this happens roughly once per day.

The tool prints this interpretation itself (`pass_gap_analysis.py:99-109`), classifying a median/half-orbit ratio in (1.2, 2.2] as "between half and one orbit — some orbits miss one station's longitude window (expected orbital mechanics, not a defect)".

**Operational consequence for the course.** With Iqaluit alone, a delayed-TM exercise had to cover an 11 h data gap; with both stations the worst case is 2.5 h, which is the regime the textbook's Ch 13 delayed-TM and 14-bit sequence-wrap arithmetic assumes. It also means the `data_dump` activity type, whose pre-condition is `ttc.in_contact == true`, is now satisfiable in Troll windows.

## 5. Mission planning commanding

**Verdict before: BROKEN.** A complete transport chain existed and delivered packets that were genuinely verified, but the *content* was wrong for the flagship activities, the scheduled time was discarded at the interface, and the on-board S11 schedule was unloadable from the ground.

**Verdict after: WORKING end-to-end for command steps**, with a stated limitation (telemetry-check steps cannot run on board).

### 5.1 The chain

1. Planner UI "Upload to MCS" → planner `/api/schedule/upload` (`smo-planner/static/index.html::uploadToMCS`).
2. Planner `_handle_upload_schedule` → MCS. Two modes now: **`s11`** (default — time-tagged on-board schedule) and **`immediate`** (legacy ProcedureRunner path). Both carry `start_time`.
3. MCS `POST /api/schedule/upload-s11` → `_handle_s11_schedule_upload`: converts `{name, start_time, steps}` into TC(11,4) packets, each `exec_time_cuc(4) + <complete embedded TC packet>`, fired at `start_time` plus cumulative `wait_s` offsets with a 1 s ordering gap between commands. `wait_for`/telemetry-check steps are reported back as **skipped** — they cannot execute on board.
4. UTC→CUC conversion: `MCSServer._utc_to_obc_cuc` anchors on the OBC time telemetry parameter `0x0309` plus the ground-clock delta, falling back to the shared `smo_common` `TIME_EPOCH` (the epoch the OBDH model boots from).
5. TC framed to the sim's TC socket (:8001 direct, or :8011→:8001 through rfsim in RF mode) → engine `_drain_tc_queue` → `_dispatch_tc` → `ServiceDispatcher._handle_s11` (subtype 4 = insert) → `tc_scheduler`.
6. Engine tick: `due_tcs = self._tc_scheduler.tick(current_cuc)` → `_dispatch_tc(tc_pkt)` → subsystem model → S1.1/S1.7 verification for the **embedded** sequence count, correlated by the MCS verification log (the embedded TC is tracked in state `SCHEDULED`).

### 5.2 The three breaks and their fixes

**Break A — scheduled time discarded (defect #44, Major).** The upload payload was `{name, steps, procedure_ref, step_by_step}` with no `start_time`; the ProcedureRunner had no concept of scheduled execution and ran whenever the operator pressed start. The planner's constraint-checked timing (daylight, contact, thermal — `constraint_checkers.py`) was thrown away at the interface.

*Fixed:* `start_time` is transmitted in both modes; the S11 mode binds execution to the planned time on board; `ProcedureRunner.load()` accepts and reports `scheduled_start_time` in `status()` so the immediate path also preserves it; the planner UI offers the choice with S11 as default.

*Evidence:* `tests/test_planner/test_upload_start_time.py` (4), `tests/test_mcs/test_s11_builder.py` (9), `tests/test_integration/test_planner_mcs_s11_chain.py` (2) — the last asserts scheduled TCs fire at the planned time in the simulator and execute with S1 verification.

**Break B — activity sequences commanded the wrong subsystems (defect #45, Major).** The engine routes S8.1 by numeric range (0–15 AOCS, 16–25 EPS, 26–39 payload, 40–49 TCS, 50–62 OBDH, 63–78 TTC). `planning/activity_types.yaml` and `imaging_planner.py` used fabricated func_ids landing in the wrong ranges:

| Activity | Was | Actually did | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| `imaging_pass` | `0x14`(20) "PAYLOAD_IMAGER_ON", `0x16`(22) "PAYLOAD_START_IMAGING" | EPS `power_line_off` no-op, then EPS `switch_load` no-op; the follow-up `wait_for payload.imaging_active` polled a parameter that does not exist and always timed out | **26** (payload set_mode, arg 2 = IMAGING) then **28** (payload capture, two big-endian float32 lat/lon); `wait_for` now polls the real `payload.mode` (param `0x0600`) |
| `data_dump` | `0x32`(50) "TTC_HIGH_RATE" | OBDH set-mode | **65** (TTC set TM rate) |
| `calibration` | `0x08` "AOCS_CALIBRATE_ST" | RW speed-bias no-op (needs a 5-byte arg) | **13** (AOCS gyro calibration) |
| `thermal_rebalance` | `0x28`(40) | forced the battery heater ON | **45** (TCS auto-mode, circuit 0) |
| `load_shedding` | `0x1A`(26) "EPS_LOAD_SHED_ENABLE" | payload set_mode | **25** (EPS emergency load shed, stage 1) |
| `fdir_recovery` | `0x1B`(27) "EPS_SAFE_MODE_INIT" | payload set_scene; AOCS safe-mode sent no mode byte | **0** with mode byte 1 (AOCS SAFE_BOOT) + **83** (EPS set-mode safe) |
| `software_upload` | S6.2 with no address/data | silent no-op | S6.2 with a writable address and data payload |

Additionally, `ProcedureRunner._build_data_hex` only read a `params` **list** (one byte each), so the imaging planner's `parameters` dict silently dropped lat/lon even before the wrong-func problem. Both the runner and the MCS S11 upload path now encode the `parameters` dict as two big-endian float32 values after the func id, matching the payload capture handler.

*Evidence:* `tests/test_planner/test_activity_funcids.py` (8) is table-driven over **all** activity types and asserts, through the real `ServiceDispatcher`, that every S8 func routes to a subsystem listed in that activity's own `requires_subsystems`, and that no `wait_for` references a nonexistent parameter. `test_planner_mcs_s11_chain.py` asserts capture lat/lon survive into the TC data field and reach the payload model.

**Break C — S11 unloadable from the ground (defect #46, Major).** The on-board side always worked. The ground side was broken twice:

- The MCS "Schedule Absolute TC" builder packed `exec_time(4) + service(1) + subtype(1) + data`, but the engine stores `data[4:]` as the deferred packet and runs `decommutate_packet` on it at fire time. A 2-byte `svc|st` blob is not a CCSDS/PUS packet, so **every** UI-scheduled command died with "TC dropped: malformed packet" — while the operator saw a successful S1.1/S1.7 for the *S11 request itself*, so it looked scheduled and verified.
- There was no ground-side UTC→OBC-CUC helper, so even a hand-crafted valid embedded packet required the operator to know the OBC's CUC epoch.

*Fixed server-side*, which covers the UI builder, procedures and any API client without touching the packing JavaScript: `MCSServer._embed_s11_packet(service, subtype, data)` detects S11.4 payloads whose embedded bytes do not already decommutate as a packet and re-wraps `svc+st+data` into a complete framed TC via `tc_manager.build_command` (own APID, sequence count, CRC), preserving the 4-byte exec time; already-complete embedded packets pass through untouched. Applied in both `_handle_pus_command` and `_proc_send_command`.

*Evidence:* `test_s11_builder.py` (9) and `test_s11_schedule_fire.py` (4), the latter including a **regression guard documenting that the old bare payload is released but never executes**.

In code subtype **4** inserts and subtype **11** is *delete-all* — the documented subtype would erase the schedule. This is a trivial doc correction and is roadmap item **N-7**.

## 6. On-board scripting

**Verdict before: ABSENT.** A repository-wide search for `obcp` and `S18` returned zero code hits. `ServiceDispatcher` implemented S1, S3, S5, S6, S8, S9, S11, S12, S13, S15, S17, S19, S20 — service 18 was not in the `service == …` chain, not in the engine's acceptance set, not in `mcs/pus_services.yaml`, not in `tc_catalog.yaml`. No example scripts existed. The three nearest substitutes each fell short:

| Substitute | Limitation |
|---|---|
| S11 time-tagged TC | Flat one-shot queue, one TC per entry, no sequence, no relative timing |
| S19 event-action | An action is exactly **one parameterless S8 function call** (`_s19_dispatch` → `_handle_s8(1, bytes([func_id]))`) — no sequences, arguments or delays |
| MCS `ProcedureRunner` | Genuinely sequenced, but runs on the **ground**: every step needs live contact and the procedure dies at LOS |

Consequently no exercise could demonstrate autonomous multi-step behaviour without ground contact. Note this was a missing capability, not a documentation lie: the textbook never claims OBCPs exist (`textbook_inventory.md` §1, autonomy row: "No OBCP / on-board control procedures or on-board scripting language is mentioned anywhere in the book").

### 6.1 What S18 now provides (defect #48)

New module `packages/smo-simulator/src/smo_simulator/obcp.py` — `ObcpManager`, a procedure store plus a per-tick step interpreter. A procedure is an ordered list of steps, each `{delay_s (relative, sim seconds), tc (complete PUS TC packet)}`. Limits: **16 procedures × 32 steps**.

**Telecommands** (`_handle_s18` in `service_dispatch.py`, registered in `dispatch()`):

| TC | Name | Data | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18,1 | `OBCP_LOAD` | serialized blob | Direct-load / replace. Replacing a running procedure aborts the run first. |
| 18,2 | `OBCP_DELETE` | `proc_id(2)` | Delete (aborts if running). |
| 18,3 | `OBCP_START` | `proc_id(2)` `[+ param(1)]` | Activate. |
| 18,4 | `OBCP_STOP` | `proc_id(2)` | Abort a run. **`proc_id = 0` stops all.** |
| 18,6 | `OBCP_STATUS_REPORT` | — | Request status → **TM(18,7)**: `count + [proc_id, status, current_step, n_steps]*`. |

Uplink load-blob layout: `proc_id(uint16) + name_len(uint8) + name + n_steps(uint8) + per step {delay_s float32 BE, tc_len uint16, tc bytes}`. `ObcpManager.encode_load_blob` builds it ground-side; `parse_load_blob` decodes on board.

**Six design properties worth noting, because they are what make it teachable rather than a toy:**

1. **Sim time.** `self._obcp_manager.tick(dt_sim)` is called from `_tick_once` immediately after the S11 scheduler, so delays are consumed in *simulated* seconds and procedures automatically respect the instructor's speed setting — one tick at 10× consumes 10 s of delay, and several steps can fire in one tick.
2. **Same dispatch path as the ground.** Steps execute through `engine._dispatch_tc`, exactly like uplinked TCs, so the bootloader allowlist, EPS power gate, bus-reachability gate, subtype validation and the full S1 verification chain all apply. *A procedure cannot do something an operator could not do.* Self-generated TCs are pre-validated against `_check_tc_acceptance` and tagged `source=OBCP` in the log, so a rejected step raises an event instead of vanishing into the malformed-packet log.
3. **Fail-stop.** A malformed or rejected step raises `OBCP_STEP_FAILED` and **aborts the remainder** rather than silently skipping ahead.
4. **Events**, via the existing `_emit_event` path and therefore S19-triggerable and alarm-stored: `0x0700 OBCP_STARTED`, `0x0701 OBCP_COMPLETED`, `0x0702 OBCP_ABORTED`, `0x0703 OBCP_STEP_FAILED`, all added to `events/event_catalog.yaml`.
5. **Breakpoints.** `get_state`/`set_state` on `ObcpManager` (JSON-safe, step TCs hex-encoded) wired into `breakpoints.py` under the `obcp` key, following the `OnboardTMStorage` pattern. A procedure mid-run when a breakpoint is saved **resumes at the same step index with the same remaining delay** after a load — so the instructor's save→inject→load workflow works across autonomy.
6. **A concurrency fix fell out of it.** The dispatcher's `threading.Lock` had to become an `RLock`, because S18 handlers emit S5 events synchronously and `_emit_event` re-enters the dispatcher via `trigger_event_action` on the same thread.

**Shipped example procedures** (`configs/eosat1/obcp/`, pre-loaded at boot like the S19 rules, so students can activate one without uploading anything):

| ID | Name | Sequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `pass_preparation` | EPS `ttc_tx` line on → PA on → deploy antenna → beacon off → set TM rate → transponder TX on |
| 2 | `safe_mode_entry` | EPS SAFE → payload mode off → payload line off → FPA cooler line off → AOCS SAFE |
| 3 | `payload_checkout` | Payload line on → STANDBY → IMAGING → capture (lat/lon) → STANDBY |

Every step uses a func_id genuinely routed by `service_dispatch.py`, asserted by `test_example_steps_use_only_implemented_funcs` — a deliberate guard against the fabricated-func_id disease found in `activity_types.yaml` (§5, defect #45).

**Ground-segment reachability.** `tc_catalog.yaml` gains `OBCP_LOAD`, `OBCP_DELETE`, `OBCP_START`, `OBCP_STOP`, `OBCP_STATUS_REPORT` (all names and field names quoted, per the defect-#40 lesson), and `mcs/pus_services.yaml` gains an `s18` stanza.

### 6.2 How to use it

Typical MCS flow, documented in full in `docs/OBCP_GUIDE.md`:

1. `OBCP_STATUS_REPORT` (no fields) — the TM(18,7) reply lists every loaded procedure with run state, confirming boot procedures 1–3 are present.
2. `OBCP_START` with `proc_id = 1` — expect S1.1/S1.3/S1.7 for the S18 TC itself, then an `OBCP_STARTED` event, then one S1 verification set per step as the procedure runs.
3. `OBCP_STATUS_REPORT` again — procedure 1 shows `status = 1` (RUNNING) with an advancing `current_step`.
4. `OBCP_STOP` with `proc_id = 1` (or `0` for all); `OBCP_DELETE` removes it from the store.

New procedures are normally added as a YAML file in `configs/eosat1/obcp/` (pre-loaded at boot) or built with `ObcpManager.encode_load_blob` from a script. A *full* ground upload via `OBCP_LOAD` needs a serialized blob containing embedded TC packets, which the catalog can only express as a raw hex `bytes` field — the same constraint the S11 `SCHEDULE_TC` command has. The catalog entry is retained for advanced use.

### 6.3 Known limitations (stated in `docs/OBCP_GUIDE.md` §6)

- No conditional logic, loops, or `wait_for <condition>` steps — a procedure is a linear timed sequence. Conditional autonomy remains S19's job (single func) or FDIR's.
- The activation `param` byte is stored and reported but not yet consumed by the interpreter (no step-level parameter substitution).
- Delays are relative and monotonic; there is no absolute-time step. To begin a procedure at a fixed CUC, time-tag the `OBCP_START` itself with S11.
- **Pre-existing, unrelated to OBCP:** S8 func 65 (set TM rate) reaches the TTC model, but the TTC tick recomputes `tm_data_rate` from `data_rate_mode`, which no telecommand can set. The high rate is therefore not reachable from the ground today, whether commanded directly or from an OBCP. This is a new finding and is roadmap item **N-8**.

*Coverage:* `tests/test_simulator/test_obcp_s18.py`, 14 tests, all acceptance criteria in defect #48 asserted.

**A note on syllabus fit.** The textbook explicitly teaches that EOSAT-1's autonomy is S11 + S19 only. S18 is therefore *sim-exceeds-book*. It should be introduced as an extension exercise (autonomous pass preparation, LOS-surviving safe-ing) rather than retrofitted into Part 2 — see roadmap **C-5**.

The stated goal — "all simulation scenario parameters clearly in a configuration file for ease of editing" — was **not met** before this session. The parameters that define how a training session behaves were Python literals across five modules with no configuration home. Two defects, both Major.

### 7.1 Defect #51 — hard-coded simulation parameters

Audited inventory (full detail in `scenario_config_inventory.md` §3):

| Module | Examples of hard-coded values |
|---|---|
| `engine.py` | tick rate 1.0 Hz (ignoring `mission.yaml network.sim_tick_hz`), separation timer 1800 s, auto-TX hold 900 s, **no clamp on `set_speed`** |
| `models/eps_basic.py` | `LOAD_SHED_VOLTAGE` 26.5 V, `LOAD_SHED_ORDER`, `OC_THRESHOLDS`, UV/OV 26.5/29.5 V, initial SoC 75 %, `ttc_rx` 5 W (ignoring the `transponder_rx_power_w` key it loads), `htr_bat`/`htr_obc`/`aocs_wheels` line loads, all eight event thresholds |
| `models/tcs_basic.py` | eclipse/sunlit environment temperatures, panel solar-heating scaling, zone conductances, warning/alarm limits, thermal-runaway rate |
| `fdir.py` | SoC shed ladder 30/20/10 % (duplicating `fault_propagation.yaml`) |
| `failure_manager.py` | gradual onset 90 s, intermittent flicker ranges |
| `scenario_engine.py` | default duration 1800 s, difficulty default, `==` tolerance 0.01 |

Worse, **four configuration sections that already existed were silently discarded** because their pydantic schemas did not declare them: `eps.yaml solar_panels` (so the deployable wings always used the 0.24 m² code default no matter what the YAML said), `eps.yaml power_lines`, `tcs.yaml thermal_coupling` and `tcs.yaml event_thresholds` (the reason earlier audits read them as "never used"), and `mission.yaml network`.

**Fixed** with a new `configs/eosat1/simulation.yaml` (**48 leaf keys**, verified) plus schema repairs:

1. **New schema and loader.** `SimulationConfig` (with `SimSpeedConfig`, `SimPhaseConfig`, `SimTTCConfig`, `SimEPSConfig` + load-shed/overcurrent/event-threshold sub-models, `SimTCSConfig`, `SimFailureConfig`, `SimScenarioDefaultsConfig`) in `smo_common/config/schemas.py`, loaded by `load_simulation_config()` following the `load_fdir_config` precedent. **Every field defaults to the previous hard-coded value**, and a missing file returns an all-defaults object — so config directories without `simulation.yaml`, including minimal test fixtures, behave exactly as before.
2. **Engine.** Tick rate honours `simulation.yaml tick_hz`, else `mission.yaml network.sim_tick_hz`, else 1 Hz; separation timer, auto-TX hold and a new `set_speed` clamp come from config; the `eps:`/`tcs:` blocks are merged into each model's config dict under a reserved `simulation` key before `create_model`.
3. **EPS model.** Load-shed order/voltage/hysteresis, overcurrent thresholds and trip time, UV/OV, initial SoC and battery temperature, the `htr_bat`/`htr_obc`/`aocs_wheels` line loads, and all eight event thresholds read from config with the module constants as fallback. The `ttc_rx` line now bills the configured `transponder_rx_power_w` instead of the hard-coded 5.0 W.
4. **TCS model.** `thermal_coupling` and `event_thresholds` are now read (the model already had matching attributes, so they were wired in rather than deleted), and the thermal environment comes from `simulation.yaml tcs.environment`.
5. **FDIR, failure manager, scenario engine.** SoC shed ladder, gradual and intermittent onset timing, and scenario defaults are configuration-driven.
6. **Schemas repaired** so existing YAML stops being dropped: `EPSConfig` gains `solar_panels`, `power_lines`, `obc_power_w`; `TCSConfig` gains `thermal_coupling`, `event_thresholds`; `MissionConfig` gains `network`.

**Ownership is explicit and non-duplicating**, stated in the file header and in `docs/SIMULATION_CONFIG.md`:

| File | Owns |
|---|---|
| `simulation.yaml` | Sim behaviour: tick rate, speed limits, phase timing, protection ladders, initial conditions, thermal environment, failure-onset timing, scenario defaults |
| `subsystems/*.yaml` | **Hardware**: array areas, battery capacity, equipment power ratings, thermal zones/heaters, param ids |
| `mission.yaml` | Identity, APIDs, ports, `network.sim_tick_hz` |
| `orbit.yaml` | TLE, T-0 epoch, canonical ground stations |
| `fdir/`, `monitoring/` | FDIR rules, fault cascades, limit checks, autonomy rules |

Where two files necessarily describe the same ladder (`simulation.yaml eps.load_shed.soc_stage_thresholds_pct` vs `fault_propagation.yaml` stage `soc_threshold`), **`simulation.yaml` wins and the engine logs a warning naming the stage on divergence** — the defect-#42 single-source principle applied to a case where deletion was not an option.

`docs/SIMULATION_CONFIG.md` gives the full key → meaning → default → *effect when changed* table, which is the form an instructor needs.

### 7.2 Defect #52 — scenario schema hardening

The loader performed **no validation**: anything the runtime did not implement was accepted at load and then quietly did nothing. An instructor saw a scenario in the list, started it, and part of it simply never happened.

Concrete dead configuration in the 31 shipped scenarios:

| File(s) | Dead content | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `gs_rf_degradation.yaml` (×2), `gs_tracking_loss.yaml` | `action: ground_failure` | Unknown action → a log line at fire time. Ground-segment failures actually live in `smo-rfsim`, which the scenario engine cannot drive. |
| `separation_sequence.yaml` | `action: start_separation`, plus `time_s:` / `type: instructor` / `value:` keys the loader never read | The first event referenced an action that never existed; the second parsed with `time_offset_s = None` and no condition, so it could never fire at all. |
| `first_contact.yaml`, `separation_sequence.yaml`, `sequential_checkout.yaml` | top-level `initial_conditions:` (phase, speed, override_passes) | Parsed by nothing — scenarios meant to begin in PRE_SEPARATION or BOOTLOADER_OPS with forced contact started in whatever state the sim happened to be in. |

There was also no protection against a mistyped failure mode, and duplicate scenario `name:` values silently overwrote each other.

**Fixed:**

1. **Load-time validation.** `SUPPORTED_ACTIONS` is now explicit (`inject`, `clear`, `set_parameter`, `message`, `set_speed`, `set_phase`). `_validate_event` rejects, and the loader **skips**, any event that uses an unsupported action, injects a failure mode the target model does not implement, is missing `subsystem`/`failure`, or has neither `time_offset_s` nor `condition` (i.e. could never fire). Non-standard `difficulty` values and duplicate scenario names are reported.
2. **Failure-mode registry that cannot drift.** `_known_failures()` introspects the model's own `inject_failure` source for `failure == "..."` comparisons, so the registry is derived from the implementation rather than maintained beside it. When a model or its source is unavailable the check is skipped rather than producing false rejections.
3. **Issues surfaced, not swallowed.** Each problem is logged and recorded in `load_issues()`; `emit_load_issues()` emits a `SCENARIO_CONFIG_INVALID` (`0x0F20`, new in `event_catalog.yaml`) S5 event per issue, called by the engine once its TM plumbing exists.
4. **`set_speed` implemented** (clamped to `simulation.yaml speed.max`) and **`set_phase` added**, routing through the instructor `set_phase` command — which is what actually arms the separation timer.
5. **`initial_conditions:` implemented** in `ScenarioEngine.start()`: `spacecraft_phase`, `speed`, `override_passes`, `soc_pct`, `power_lines`, `params` and `failures` (pre-injected and tracked, so they clear when the scenario ends).
6. **Scenario YAMLs repaired rather than deleted.** `separation_sequence.yaml` now uses `time_offset_s` with `set_phase`/`set_speed` and its `initial_conditions` supply the 95 % SoC its own briefing promises. The three `ground_failure` events became explicit instructor-cue `message` events naming the rfsim injection to perform, with a comment explaining that the ground segment is a separate simulator.
7. **An engine bug found while wiring this:** the instructor `set_phase` command set `_sep_timer` *before* calling `_enter_bootloader_mode()`, which zeroes it — so commanding phase 1 left the separation timer at 0 and it expired on the next tick. The assignment now happens after, and the timer arms correctly at 1800 s.

*Evidence:* `tests/test_simulator/test_simulation_config.py`, 15 tests including `TestScenarioSchemaHardening`; **all 31 shipped scenarios load with zero issues**.

### 7.3 What remains

- `set_parameter` still requires numeric or hex parameter ids; symbolic names (`eps.bat_soc`) are rejected because there is no name→id registry. The registry exists in `subsystems/*.yaml param_ids` and could be built at load — roadmap **L-4**.
- The scenario `condition:` grammar is still a single `"<param_id> <op> <value>"` with `<`, `>`, `==`, `<=`, `>=`. Adequate, but only 2 of 31 scenarios use it.
- `ScenarioConfig`/`load_scenarios()` in `smo_common` remain an unused parallel schema (register DI-09 family) — either wire them or delete them.
- The remaining literals identified in `scenario_config_inventory.md` §3.7 (AOCS orbit-phase period 5700 s vs the TLE's 5669 s, eclipse penumbra 50 km, contact/eclipse scan windows, star-tracker blinding geometry, wheel friction) are lower priority and would sit in an `aocs.*` block in the same file.

## 9. Power and battery analysis

**Question: will the battery drain flat under standard operation?** Answered quantitatively, and the answer changed what the simulator ships with.

### 9.1 Method

A standalone reproduction of the simulator's **own** EPS arithmetic — not textbook equations — integrated over 72 h at dt = 1 s (`/home/claude/work/power_sim/eps_sim.py`; raw results in `results.json`; plots `soc_vs_time.png`, `gen_vs_load.png`). The model was extracted line-by-line into `power_model_extract.md` first, so the reproduction is auditable:

- `ΔSoC% = net_W·dt/4320` (120 Wh, **no coulombic efficiency**); `OCV = 21.5 + 7.7·SoC/100` V; `bat_i = net/ocv`; `v_loaded = ocv − bat_i·0.05`; bus = `min(29, bat_v)`; noise `N(0,1)` W on consumption, ±0.5 % per panel on generation, 0.02/0.01 V on voltages (seed 42). The sim's sign convention makes bus voltage *rise* ~0.07 V under discharge; reproduced as-is rather than corrected, so the comparison is like-for-like.
- **Loads** are line-based, using the wattages the tick code actually bills: obc 15 + ttc_rx 5 + wheels 6 + heaters 6+4 (billed at full power regardless of thermostat duty — a sim quirk, reproduced) = **36 W nominal platform**; ttc_tx +20 W; payload 8 W standby / 45 W imaging; FPA cooler 15 W.
- **Generation**: the 6-panel CSS model, wings deployed, β = 0. The sun sweeps the body X-Y plane at 360°/5700 s; per face `0.295·1361·area·max(0,cosθ)·0.97`. Orbit-average generation is **53.8 W** when the eclipse is centred on the X-panel lobe but only **46.0 W** when centred on the Y-wing lobe. Because the AOCS sweep (5700 s) and the propagator's eclipse period (5669 s) beat against each other, the sim drifts ~90° between these alignments within days — **so the budget must close at 46 W**, not 53.8 W. This is the single most important methodological point in the analysis.
- **Contacts**: Iqaluit on 2 of 3 orbits (≈10 passes/day), 600 s pass, TX billed AOS→LOS+900 s. P3/P4 add Troll half an orbit later.
- **Imaging duty** (defensible per `activity_types.yaml imaging_pass`): payload line on at 8 W standby, 3 × 120 s captures per orbit at 45 W on the day-side arc, cooler 15 W for 1300 s bracketing them → **+15.1 W** orbit-average.
- **Protections** modelled: the in-model bus-voltage shed; the working `fdir.yaml` rules (SoC<20 payload off, SoC<15 EPS-safe, bus<26 EPS-safe, bus<24 AOCS-safe); and the `fault_propagation.yaml` 30/20/10 stages run **both as-is (dead) and as-intended**, so the effect of the bug could be isolated.

### 9.2 Before

| Profile | Gen avg (best/worst align) | Load avg | Budget Wh/orbit | Min SoC | Shed stages tripped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** nominal, Iqaluit only | 53.8 / 46.0 W | 39.6 W | **+22 / +10** | 70.7 % | none — *but* starting at eclipse entry gives min 55.7 % and a full 4-line cascade at t = 0.7 h |
| **P2** + imaging duty | 53.8 / 46.0 W | 53.3 W | **+0.8 / −11.6** | 63.7 % | full 4-line cascade in the **first eclipse** (t = 1.07 h) → imaging permanently dead, no auto-restore |
| **P3** + Troll (two stations) | 53.8 / 46.0 W | 56.8 W | **−4.7 / −17.1** | 63.7 % | same cascade at t = 1.07 h |
| **P4** sustained science | 53.8 / 46.0 W | 103.1 W | **−78** | 46.5 % | full cascade at t = 0.54 h, before the first eclipse ends |

**Verdict on the literal question:** the battery does *not* flatline under standard operation — the bus-voltage shed and the `fdir.yaml` rules always catch it. Flatlining requires *all* protections defeated (1.73 h in P4, 26.3 h in P3).

**But four things were wrong:**

1. **The shed threshold sat inside the normal operating band.** `LOAD_SHED_VOLTAGE = 26.5 V` is the battery OCV at **64.9 % SoC** — above the normal eclipse trough from a 75 % start. P2–P4 all stripped payload, cooler, TX and wheels in the first eclipse, and even nominal P1 did if the session began near eclipse entry. With no auto-restore, science was dead about an hour into every session unless someone manually re-enabled every line. **Load shedding was routine behaviour, not a contingency — which destroys its training value, because a trainee cannot distinguish it from normal operation.**
2. **The imaging mission was energy-marginal and the two-station fix made it negative.** P2 is +0.8 Wh/orbit at favourable alignment and −11.6 at the alignment the sim drifts into within a day or two; P3 (after defect #41) is net-negative at *both* alignments. Fixing the contact model broke the power budget — a genuine coupled-system finding that neither review item would have found alone.
3. **The `ttc_tx` shed stage was defeated during passes.** The 900 s auto-TX hold re-arms on every accepted TC, forcing the line back on, so shedding never actually saved TX energy in contact.
4. **The SoC ladder was dead code.** `engine.py::_tick_fdir_advanced` read `getattr(eps._state, 'soc', 100.0)`; `EPSState` has no `soc` attribute (it is `bat_soc_pct`), so the manager was fed 100.0 on every tick, `get_required_stage()` always returned NORMAL, no `LOAD_SHED_ACTIVATED` event was ever emitted, and the three configured stages in `fault_propagation.yaml` with their `subsystem_commands` were loaded, registered and never executed. In the shipped configuration the bug made **zero difference** in every profile, because the 26.5 V shed fired first — it was completely masked. It becomes the load-bearing protection layer the moment the shed voltage is corrected.

Also found while applying fixes: the pydantic schemas silently dropped `eps.yaml solar_panels`, so **the wings were running on the 0.24 m² code default regardless of the YAML** (§7.1).

| Profile | Trend Wh/orbit (best / worst align) | Min SoC | Sheds |

Absolute end-of-run SoC is not a useful metric here, because SoC saturates at 100 % on each sunlit arc; **the per-orbit minimum converging is the correct closure criterion**, and it does.

### 9.5 Margins and what still trips the ladder (training value preserved)

Standard operations (P1–P3) now close with positive margin per orbit **at both sun alignments** with zero shed events, and the worst-case per-orbit minimum SoC (71.1 %) sits **more than 25 points clear** of the 45.5 %-equivalent shed band. Shedding is therefore no longer routine.

It remains fully reachable and demonstrable:

- **Sustained-science abuse (P4)** — continuous imaging with cooler and TX — drives the bus below 25.0 V and triggers FDIR `bus < 26 V` → EPS safe mode at t ≈ 0.9 h.
- **Injected failures**: `bus_short` (+80 W), `solar_array_partial` / `solar_panel_loss` / `solar_array_total_loss`, and `bat_cell_failure` (−3.7 V) all still drive the full shed and FDIR ladder.
- **The repaired SoC ladder is now the load-bearing layer** in shed-defeated scenarios: with the voltage shed disabled, the working 30/20/10 stages intervene **3.2 h earlier** than the FDIR-only path in P3 and preserve **+10.2 points (~12 Wh)** of reserve (min SoC 29.4 % vs 19.2 %).
- `TestSocLoadShedStagesTrip` asserts an ordered stage 1→2→3 trip with generation zeroed, and carries a direct regression on the dead attribute read.

### 9.6 Caveats and follow-ups

- β = 0 is the worst-case eclipse fraction (0.384, ≈36.2 min of a 94.5 min orbit). At β = 20–40° margins improve slightly.
- **Pre-wing-deploy generation is ~22 W sunlit average, which cannot sustain even the 30 W commissioned platform.** LEOP is only survivable because the AOCS-off fallback array model over-generates (~245 W sunlit, a legacy path applying whenever AOCS is off — i.e. all of phases 0–3). **Wing deploy must precede wheel and heater commissioning in the procedures**, and the fallback model should be retired (roadmap **N-9**); until it is, LEOP power exercises are not physically meaningful.
- Heater lines are billed at full 10 W regardless of thermostat duty, so real margin is slightly better than modelled.
- Not needed: battery growth (120 Wh gives a 22-point SoC eclipse swing at worst load, which is fine once the per-orbit trend is positive). `platform_idle_power_w: 70.0` remains loaded and unused — it should be deleted or wired, and it is the origin of a **false claim in the textbook** (§10, and `textbook_vs_sim.md` §3 row 1).
- Optional: lower the `fdir.yaml` bus rules from 26/24 V to 24.5/23.5 V so the repaired 30/20/10 SoC stages layer *beneath* the voltage shed rather than being pre-empted by it.
